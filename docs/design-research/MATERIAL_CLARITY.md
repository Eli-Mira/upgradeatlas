# Material Clarity — visual philosophy

Give physical objects the strongest visual presence. Treat metal, proportion, edges and light as the expressive layer; keep the surrounding interface composed. The work should feel meticulously crafted through alignment and restraint, not through accumulating effects.

Use cool light neutrals and near-black ink to separate content without enclosing every element in a card. A single controlled accent marks decisions. Color hierarchy is the product of careful calibration; it must remain legible when the imagery becomes dramatic.

Let typography establish confidence through weight, rhythm and scale. Arabic must receive the same painstaking attention as Latin, with its own line-height and optical balance. Information should be readable at a glance, then reward deeper inspection.

Motion explains change rather than demanding attention. Material imagery can be cinematic while controls remain predictable. Expert execution is visible in the details: stable geometry, consistent comparison alignment, comfortable spacing and nothing decorative competing with a decision.
